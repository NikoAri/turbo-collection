# A Domain term is written as its terminology section writes it, capitals included

**Status:** Accepted **Date:** 2026-10-25

[`language-requirement.md`](../../specs/language-requirement.md) gained
R-LANG-23: wherever a normative document means one of its defined terms, it
writes that term exactly as its own terminology section does, capitals included,
and a word used in its ordinary sense takes no capital. "A TC Copy that lacks a
Content File" uses a term; "a file a person removed" uses a word.

A capital shows a reader where a sentence leans on a definition, without looking
anything up. R-LANG-6 already gives each concept one name; this makes that name
visible. A terminology section's entry is the one place a term's form is
decided, so no second list of capitalized words exists to drift from it.

This was house style since 2026-10-06, followed in new text and stated in no
normative document. Writing it into the authoring standard is what binds every
author to it, and not only those who knew it.

## Rejected

- **House style left unwritten.** A rule no document states cannot be checked in
  review, and a later author has no way to learn it.
- **Lowercase throughout**, as ordinary English writes such words. A defined
  concept and that same word in its plain sense then look alike, and a reader
  must guess which one a sentence means.
- **Listing it among checker-enforceable rules** in `version-requirement.md`
  R-PUB-10 step 3. Whether a word carries its defined sense takes judgment,
  which no spelling or pattern check has.
